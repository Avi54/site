The Goldsmiths' Alchemy: The Origin of Paper Money

Where did paper money actually come from? In Princes of the Yen, Richard Werner answers this by tracing it to a simple receipt slip, a piece of paper a medieval goldsmith handed over for gold left in safekeeping. That receipt is principally an ancestor to the bank note, and closely tied to the origins of paper money in Europe. From Storage Slip to Money Goldsmiths had the strongest vaults in town, hence people deposited their gold with them and in exchange were given a receipt as proof of the deposit. Carrying gold around town to settle payments was risky and cumbersome. As a result over time instead of withdrawing gold to make a payment, they just handed over the receipt itself, trusting that whoever received it could redeem it for gold.
